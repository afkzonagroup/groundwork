# Legal basis

This page sets out what the law says about AI literacy, quoted from the
official texts on EUR-Lex, and how Groundwork relates to it. It is general
information, not legal advice. Texts were read on 8 November 2026.

## Article 4: AI literacy

### Current wording (from 27 July 2026)

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Article 1, point (5), replaced Article 4 of the
AI Act. Article 4(1) now reads:

> "Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to support the
> development of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with
> the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf, taking into account
> their technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the
> context the AI systems are to be used in, and considering the persons or
> groups of persons on whom the AI systems are to be used. This obligation
> does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of
> AI literacy of any individual."

Article 4(2) requires the Commission and the Member States to "support and
facilitate the efforts of providers and deployers of AI systems, in
particular SMEs", and the Commission to "publish practical examples of how
to comply with that obligation on the single information platform referred
to in Article 62(3), point (b)". Article 4(3) asks the AI Board to adopt
recommendations, taking into account European competence frameworks.

**Date.** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was published in the Official Journal on
24 July 2026 and, under its Article 4, entered into force "on the third day
following that of its publication", that is on 27 July 2026. The
consolidated text on EUR-Lex carries the same date. The Commission's
questions and answers say the amendments "entered into force in mid-July
2026"; this project follows the Official Journal.

### Original wording (2 February 2025 to 26 July 2026)

> "Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to ensure, to
> their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and
> other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their
> behalf, taking into account their technical knowledge, experience,
> education and training and the context the AI systems are to be used in,
> and considering the persons or groups of persons on whom the AI systems are
> to be used."

Article 4 is in Chapter I. Under Article 113, third paragraph, point (a),
Chapters I and II apply from 2 February 2025.

### Definition (Article 3, point (56))

> "'AI literacy' means skills, knowledge and understanding that allow
> providers, deployers and affected persons, taking into account their
> respective rights and obligations in the context of this Regulation, to
> make an informed deployment of AI systems, as well as to gain awareness
> about the opportunities and risks of AI and possible harm it can cause"

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 did not change this definition.

### Recital 20

Recital 20 explains that AI literacy "should equip providers, deployers and
affected persons with the necessary notions to make informed decisions
regarding AI systems", and that those notions "may vary with regard to the
relevant context".

## What the law does and does not require

- **It requires measures.** Article 4 requires providers and deployers to
  take measures that support AI literacy, fitted to the people and the
  context.
- **It does not require a certificate, an exam or a specific course.** The
  text names none. The Commission's questions and answers say: "There is no
  need for a certificate."
- **It does not require a specific level for any individual.** The current
  Article 4(1) says so explicitly.
- **Records are good evidence practice, not a legal form.** The AI Act does
  not prescribe records for Article 4. The Commission's questions and
  answers say organisations "can keep an internal record of trainings and/or
  other guiding initiatives".
- **Examples are not a safe harbour.** The Commission says of its living
  repository that it "does not automatically grant presumption of compliance
  with Article 4".
- **Penalties.** Article 99(1) leaves "the rules on penalties and other
  enforcement measures" for infringements of the AI Act to the Member
  States. Article 99(3) to (5) set maximum fines for specific provisions;
  Article 4 is not among them.

## How Groundwork maps to the law

| Provision | What Groundwork does | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| [Art. 4(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727#art_4): measures for AI literacy | Provides an open course that an organisation can use as one measure, with versions for all staff, people building or buying AI, and managers | Does not decide which measures are enough for an organisation; does not cover the organisation's own tools and rules |
| Art. 4(1): "taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the context" | Role variants; placeholders for the organisation's name and AI contact; content can be changed and extended (CC BY 4.0) | Does not assess people's existing knowledge |
| Art. 4(1): no specific level guaranteed | Quizzes help people check their understanding; the pass mark is configurable | Quizzes are not an exam and not a certificate |
| [Art. 3(56)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727#art_3): skills, knowledge, understanding, opportunities, risks, possible harm | Modules on what AI is and how it fails, data protection, checking outputs, transparency, prohibited practices and escalation | |
| Evidence of measures (good practice) | Tamper-evident records, CSV/JSON/xAPI export, SCORM 1.2 and xAPI for LMSs | Records are not required by law and are not a legal form |
