## Learning objectives

- Explain in your own words what AI literacy means in the AI Act.
- Say who the AI literacy duty in Article 4 applies to.
- Tell the difference between what the law requires (measures) and what it does not require (a certificate or a specific course).
- Know where to find the official texts and the European Commission's guidance.

## What the law says

The EU's Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the "AI Act") sets rules for AI systems placed on the market or used in the EU. Article 4 is about AI literacy.

Since 27 July 2026, Article 4(1) reads:

::: note
"Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf, taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the context the AI systems are to be used in, and considering the persons or groups of persons on whom the AI systems are to be used. This obligation does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual."
:::

This wording comes from Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the "Digital Omnibus on AI"), which replaced Article 4. It was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on the third day after publication. From 2 February 2025 until then, the original Article 4 asked providers and deployers to "take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy" of the same people.

Article 4 is in Chapter I of the AI Act. Chapters I and II apply from 2 February 2025 (Article 113, point (a)).

## What AI literacy means

Article 3, point (56) defines AI literacy as "skills, knowledge and understanding that allow providers, deployers and affected persons, taking into account their respective rights and obligations in the context of this Regulation, to make an informed deployment of AI systems, as well as to gain awareness about the opportunities and risks of AI and possible harm it can cause".

In plain words: knowing enough about the AI you work with to use it well, to notice when it goes wrong, and to understand who could be harmed.

## Who it applies to

- A **provider** develops an AI system, or has one developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark (Article 3, point (3)).
- A **deployer** uses an AI system under its authority, except for personal, non-professional use (Article 3, point (4)).

Most organisations that use AI tools at work are deployers. The duty covers their staff and "other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf", for example contractors.

## What it does and does not require

- The law requires **measures** that support AI literacy. Taking this course can be one such measure.
- The law does **not** require a certificate, an exam or a specific course. The European Commission's questions and answers on AI literacy say: "There is no need for a certificate."
- The amended Article 4 says the duty "does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual".
- Keeping a record of training is **good evidence practice**, not a legal form. The Commission's questions and answers say organisations "can keep an internal record of trainings and/or other guiding initiatives".

The Commission and the Member States must support providers and deployers, in particular SMEs, and the Commission must publish practical examples (Article 4(2)). The Commission also keeps a living repository of AI literacy practices. It says that following those practices does not automatically mean an organisation complies with Article 4.

::: role managers
### For managers

Article 4 asks you to take into account the technical knowledge, experience, education and training of your people, the context in which the AI systems are used, and the people the systems are used on. A useful AI literacy programme therefore starts with a short inventory:

- Which AI systems do we use or provide, and for what?
- Who uses them, and what do they already know?
- Who could be affected by the results?

This course covers general knowledge. Add briefings on the specific tools your teams use and on your own rules. Keep a simple record of what you did, when, and for whom. The AI Act does not prescribe its form.

Article 99(1) leaves penalties and other enforcement measures for infringements of the AI Act to the Member States. Unlike the prohibitions in Article 5, Article 99 sets no fixed maximum fine for Article 4.
:::

::: role builders
### For people building or buying AI

If your organisation develops an AI system and offers it under its own name, it is a provider of that system, and the AI literacy duty also covers the people who build and operate it. When you buy or configure an AI system for use at work, your organisation is a deployer. Either way, the context of use and the people the system is used on matter for what your colleagues need to know.
:::

## Quiz

### Question ai-literacy-at-work-1
What does Article 4 of the AI Act require of providers and deployers of AI systems?

- [ ] That every employee passes an accredited AI exam.
- [x] That they take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and others using AI systems on their behalf.
- [ ] That they appoint a certified AI officer.
- [ ] That they stop using AI until all staff have a certificate.

Explanation: Article 4 requires measures. It does not require an exam, a certificate or a specific job title.

### Question ai-literacy-at-work-2
Does the AI Act require a certificate to show that staff are AI literate?

- [ ] Yes, issued by a national authority.
- [ ] Yes, but only for managers.
- [x] No. The law requires measures, not a certificate.

Explanation: The European Commission's questions and answers say: "There is no need for a certificate." Records of training are useful evidence, but the law does not prescribe a form.

### Question ai-literacy-at-work-3
Who is a deployer under the AI Act?

- [ ] Only companies that build AI models.
- [x] Anyone using an AI system under their authority, except for personal, non-professional use.
- [ ] Only public authorities.
- [ ] Anyone who has heard of AI.

Explanation: Article 3, point (4) defines a deployer as a person or body "using an AI system under its authority except where the AI system is used in the course of a personal non-professional activity". Most organisations that use AI tools at work are deployers.

### Question ai-literacy-at-work-4
Which people does the AI literacy duty cover?

- [ ] Only full-time employees in IT.
- [ ] Only customers.
- [x] Staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on the organisation's behalf, such as contractors.

Explanation: Article 4 covers "staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf".

### Question ai-literacy-at-work-5
Roles: managers

A manager plans AI literacy measures for a team. What does Article 4 ask them to take into account?

- [ ] Only the price of the training.
- [x] People's technical knowledge, experience, education and training, the context of use, and the people the AI systems are used on.
- [ ] Only the number of AI tools the team uses.

Explanation: These are the factors listed in Article 4(1). They mean the measures should fit the people and the use, not be one size for everyone.

### Question ai-literacy-at-work-6
Roles: builders

Your company develops an AI system and sells it under its own brand. What is it under the AI Act?

- [x] A provider of that AI system.
- [ ] Only a deployer.
- [ ] Neither; the AI Act does not apply to sellers.

Explanation: Article 3, point (3): a provider develops an AI system, or has it developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark.
