## Learning objectives

- Describe in plain words the AI practices that Article 5 of the AI Act prohibits.
- Recognise a prohibited practice that could come up at work, such as emotion recognition of employees.
- Know since when the prohibitions apply and how serious the penalties are.
- Know that this overview is a summary and that the article text decides.

## Since when

The original prohibitions in Article 5(1), points (a), (b) and (c) to (h), apply from 2 February 2025 (Article 113, point (a)). Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 inserted two further prohibitions after point (b), points (ba) and (bb), which apply from 2 December 2026. Breaking a prohibition can lead to fines of up to EUR 35 000 000 or, for an undertaking, up to 7 % of its total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher (Article 99(3)). For SMEs, including start-ups, the lower of the two applies (Article 99(6)).

## The prohibited practices in plain words

The AI Act prohibits placing on the market, putting into service or using AI systems that:

- **(a) manipulate** people with subliminal techniques or purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques, materially distorting their behaviour in a way that causes or is reasonably likely to cause significant harm;
- **(b) exploit vulnerabilities** due to age, disability or a specific social or economic situation, in the same harmful way;
- **(c) score people socially**: evaluate or classify people over time based on their social behaviour or personal characteristics, where the score leads to unjustified or disproportionate detrimental treatment, or to detrimental treatment in unrelated contexts;
- **(d) predict crime from profiling**: assess the risk that a person will commit a crime based solely on profiling or on personality traits (with an exception for supporting a human assessment based on objective and verifiable facts);
- **(e) scrape faces**: create or expand facial recognition databases through untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage;
- **(f) infer emotions at work or in education**: infer the emotions of a person in the workplace or in education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons;
- **(g) categorise people by sensitive traits from biometric data**: deduce or infer race, political opinions, trade union membership, religious or philosophical beliefs, sex life or sexual orientation from biometric data (with exceptions for labelling or filtering lawfully acquired biometric datasets and for law enforcement);
- **(h) use real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces for law enforcement**, except in narrowly defined cases with prior authorisation and safeguards.

From 2 December 2026, also AI systems that:

- **(ba) generate intimate images of real people without consent**: generate or manipulate realistic images, videos, audio or similar material of an identifiable person's intimate parts or of an identifiable person engaged in sexually explicit activities, without that person's freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous and explicit consent;
- **(bb) generate child sexual abuse material**, except where a "without right" defence applies under national law.

Article 5(1a) and (1b) explain when points (ba) and (bb) apply to placing such systems on the market and to using them.

## At work

The most likely situations at work are:

- a tool that claims to read employees' emotions from their faces, voices or typing, for example in video calls or call centres (point (f));
- marketing techniques that use AI to exploit people's vulnerabilities (points (a) and (b));
- image tools used to create sexual images of colleagues or other real people (point (ba)).

If you see anything like this, do not use it, and raise a concern (module 7). This overview leaves out details and exceptions; the article text decides.

::: role builders
### For people building or buying AI

Check every new AI use and every new supplier against Article 5 before you start. Watch for features that infer emotions of employees or students, for example "engagement" or "sentiment" scores from cameras or microphones in meetings or classrooms. For generative image tools, check that the supplier has safeguards against generating intimate images of real people.
:::

::: role managers
### For managers

Do not buy or allow tools that infer the emotions of your employees, except for medical or safety reasons. If a supplier offers "mood" or "engagement" scores from cameras or voices, ask your legal or compliance contact before you go further.
:::

## Quiz

### Question prohibited-practices-1
A supplier offers software that analyses employees' faces in video meetings to score how engaged and happy they are. What does the AI Act say?

- [ ] This is allowed if employees agree in their contracts.
- [x] Using AI to infer emotions of people in the workplace is prohibited, except for medical or safety reasons.
- [ ] This is allowed if the scores are kept private.

Explanation: Article 5(1), point (f) prohibits AI systems that infer emotions of a natural person in the workplace and in education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons.

### Question prohibited-practices-2
Since when do the original prohibitions in Article 5(1) of the AI Act apply, for example the ban on inferring emotions at work (point (f))?

- [x] 2 February 2025.
- [ ] 2 August 2027.
- [ ] They do not apply yet.

Explanation: Chapter II, which contains Article 5, applies from 2 February 2025 (Article 113, point (a)). Only the two prohibitions added by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, points (ba) and (bb), apply later, from 2 December 2026.

### Question prohibited-practices-3
What is the maximum fine for breaking a prohibition in Article 5?

- [ ] EUR 15 000 in all cases.
- [x] Up to EUR 35 000 000 or up to 7 % of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.
- [ ] There is no fine; only a warning.

Explanation: Article 99(3) of the AI Act. For SMEs, including start-ups, the lower of the two amounts applies (Article 99(6)).

### Question prohibited-practices-4
A colleague uses an AI image tool to create a realistic nude image of another colleague without consent. Which statement is correct?

- [ ] This is a private matter and not relevant at work.
- [x] It is seriously harmful, can be unlawful, and should be reported at once.
- [ ] It is acceptable if the image is not shared.

Explanation: It harms the person depicted and can be unlawful under other laws already. In addition, Article 5(1), point (ba) of the AI Act, added by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, prohibits AI systems that generate such material without consent from 2 December 2026, under the conditions in Article 5(1a). Report it.

### Question prohibited-practices-5
A system builds a facial recognition database by collecting photos from social media without targeting specific people. Under the AI Act this is:

- [ ] Allowed if the photos are public.
- [x] Prohibited: creating or expanding facial recognition databases through untargeted scraping.
- [ ] Allowed for marketing purposes.

Explanation: Article 5(1), point (e).

### Question prohibited-practices-6
Roles: builders

A product owner wants to add a "classroom attention score" based on webcam analysis of students' emotions to a learning platform used in schools. What should you do?

- [ ] Build it, since it helps teachers.
- [x] Stop and raise it: inferring emotions in education institutions is prohibited except for medical or safety reasons.
- [ ] Build it, but only for older students.

Explanation: Article 5(1), point (f) covers education institutions as well as workplaces.

### Question prohibited-practices-7
Roles: managers

A vendor offers call-centre software that rates agents' moods from their voices to plan coaching. What should you do?

- [ ] Buy it if the price is right.
- [x] Do not go ahead; inferring employees' emotions is prohibited unless for medical or safety reasons. Ask your legal or compliance contact.
- [ ] Use it only for one month as a trial.

Explanation: Article 5(1), point (f). A trial is still use.
