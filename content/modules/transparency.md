## Learning objectives

- Name the four situations Article 50 of the AI Act covers.
- Say whether a duty falls on the provider or the deployer of an AI system.
- Recognise a deep fake and know when it must be disclosed.
- Avoid overstating what Article 50 requires.

## Four situations

Article 50 of the AI Act applies from 2 August 2026, the AI Act's general date of application (Article 113). It covers four situations:

1. **AI that talks with people.** Providers must design AI systems that interact directly with people so that the people are told they are interacting with an AI system, unless that is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person (Article 50(1)).
2. **AI-generated content.** Providers of AI systems that generate synthetic audio, images, video or text must make sure the outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated, with some exceptions such as assistive editing (Article 50(2)).
3. **Emotion recognition and biometric categorisation.** Deployers of these systems must inform the people exposed to them (Article 50(3)).
4. **Deep fakes and public-interest text.** Deployers of AI that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake must disclose that the content is artificially generated or manipulated. Deployers that publish AI-generated or manipulated text to inform the public on matters of public interest must disclose that too, unless the text has undergone human review or editorial control and someone holds editorial responsibility for it (Article 50(4)).

The information must be given "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure", and it must meet the applicable accessibility requirements (Article 50(5)).

## What is a deep fake

The AI Act defines a deep fake as AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful (Article 3, point (60)). For evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work, the duty is limited to disclosing the existence of the generated or manipulated content in a way that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work (Article 50(4)).

## What this means for you

- If you publish an AI-generated or manipulated photo, video or audio clip of a real person, place or event that could pass as real, say clearly that it is AI-generated or manipulated.
- If {organisation} uses a chatbot with customers, people must be told they are talking to an AI system, unless that is obvious. Do not remove or hide that notice.
- Do not strip machine-readable marks from AI-generated files.
- Your organisation may have stricter rules, for example labelling all AI-assisted marketing. Follow them.

## What Article 50 does not say

Article 50 does not require a label on every email or document you drafted with AI help. It covers the situations above. Other laws, such as consumer protection and data protection law, and your organisation's own rules can still require transparency in other cases (Article 50(6) says Article 50 is without prejudice to other transparency obligations).

::: role builders
### For people building or buying AI

- If your organisation provides a chatbot or a content generator, the Article 50(1) and 50(2) duties fall on it as provider. Build the notice and the marking into the product.
- Providers of generative systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to comply with the marking duty in Article 50(2) (Article 111, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).
- If you buy such a system, check that the supplier meets these duties and that your configuration does not switch them off.
:::

::: role managers
### For managers

Decide who signs off on publishing AI-generated images, audio and video, and make the disclosure part of that step. Keep it simple: a clear line of text near the content is often enough.
:::

## Quiz

### Question transparency-1
Your company's website chatbot is an AI system. What does Article 50(1) require?

- [x] People must be told they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious.
- [ ] The chatbot must be switched off outside office hours.
- [ ] Every answer must be checked by a person before it is shown.

Explanation: Article 50(1) requires that people are informed they are interacting with an AI system, unless that is obvious from the point of view of a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person.

### Question transparency-2
Marketing wants to post an AI-generated video of a real politician appearing to endorse your product. Apart from other legal and ethical problems, what does Article 50(4) require if it is published?

- [ ] Nothing, if the video looks good.
- [x] Disclosure that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated.
- [ ] Only a note in the company's internal files.

Explanation: Such a video is a deep fake. Deployers must disclose that it has been artificially generated or manipulated. Publishing it could also break other laws, for example on misleading advertising or personality rights.

### Question transparency-3
You used an AI tool to improve the wording of an internal email. Does Article 50 require you to label the email as AI-generated?

- [ ] Yes, every AI-assisted text needs a label.
- [x] No. Article 50 does not cover this case, but follow any stricter rule your organisation has.
- [ ] Only if the email is longer than one page.

Explanation: Article 50 covers specific situations. An internal email drafted with AI help is not one of them.

### Question transparency-4
When must the information required by Article 50 be given?

- [ ] Within 30 days.
- [x] In a clear and distinguishable manner, at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure.
- [ ] Only when someone asks.

Explanation: Article 50(5) of the AI Act.

### Question transparency-5
Which statement is correct?

- [ ] The AI Act's transparency duties apply only to public authorities.
- [x] Some Article 50 duties fall on providers and others on deployers.
- [ ] Article 50 replaces all other transparency rules.

Explanation: Paragraphs 1 and 2 are provider duties; paragraphs 3 and 4 are deployer duties. Article 50(6) says other transparency obligations still apply.

### Question transparency-6
Roles: builders

Your company provides an image-generation feature. Which duty in Article 50 applies to the outputs?

- [ ] None, because images are not text.
- [x] The outputs must be marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.
- [ ] Each user must sign a declaration.

Explanation: Article 50(2) applies to providers of AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text content.

### Question transparency-7
Roles: managers

How can you make sure AI-generated media is disclosed when your team publishes it?

- [ ] Trust each person to remember.
- [x] Make disclosure part of the sign-off step before publishing.
- [ ] Ban all images from publications.

Explanation: A fixed step in the publishing process is more reliable than memory.
