## Learning objectives

- Recognise situations involving AI that you should report.
- Take the right first steps when something goes wrong.
- Know who to tell in {organisation}.
- Explain why early reports matter, including for legal deadlines.

## When to raise a concern

Raise a concern when, for example:

- personal or confidential data may have gone into a tool or to people it should not;
- AI output could harm someone, for example an unfair decision about a customer or applicant;
- an AI system behaves in a way nobody expected, or gives results that look systematically wrong for one group of people;
- you see a use that might be prohibited (module 6), such as emotion recognition of employees;
- AI-generated media of a real person is about to be published without disclosure, or is being used to harass someone;
- someone asks you to switch off a safeguard, a notice or a human check "just this once";
- a customer or member of the public complains about an AI decision or an AI interaction.

If you are unsure, report it. A concern that turns out to be nothing costs little.

## What to do in the moment

1. **Stop** using the output or the tool for that task, if you can do so safely.
2. **Do not spread it.** Do not forward personal data or harmful content to more people than needed.
3. **Note the facts:** what tool, when, what you put in, what came out, who may be affected. Keep a copy only if your organisation's rules allow it.
4. **Tell {ai-contact}.** For a possible personal data breach, also tell your data protection contact.
5. **Do not try to fix it alone** if it affects other people, customers or systems.

## Why speed matters

Some duties have short deadlines. A personal data breach must, where feasible, be notified to the supervisory authority within 72 hours of the organisation becoming aware of it, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to people (Article 33 GDPR). For high-risk AI systems, the AI Act has its own reporting duties for serious incidents: providers report to the market surveillance authorities (Article 73), and deployers who identify a serious incident must immediately inform first the provider, and then the importer or distributor and the market surveillance authorities (Article 26(5)). Your organisation can only meet these duties if people report early.

::: role builders
### For people building or buying AI

Make it easy for users to report problems from inside the tool, and route reports to someone who can act. For high-risk AI systems you provide, set up the serious-incident process required by Article 73 before the system goes live. Keep logs that let you investigate, without keeping more personal data than you need.
:::

::: role managers
### For managers

Make reporting safe. Thank people who raise concerns, even when the concern turns out to be nothing, and never treat a report as a mistake to be punished. Make sure everyone knows who {ai-contact} is and how to reach them.
:::

## Quiz

### Question escalation-1
You notice that an AI screening tool rejects almost every applicant from one town. What should you do?

- [ ] Nothing. The tool is probably right.
- [ ] Change the tool's settings yourself.
- [x] Stop relying on the results for those decisions and report it to {ai-contact}.

Explanation: Results that look systematically wrong for one group can mean bias. Stop relying on them and report it so the people responsible can investigate.

### Question escalation-2
A colleague asks you to turn off the "you are chatting with an AI" notice on the customer chatbot because it "puts customers off". What do you do?

- [ ] Turn it off, since the colleague asked.
- [x] Do not turn it off, and raise it with {ai-contact}.
- [ ] Turn it off only on weekends.

Explanation: The notice can be required by Article 50(1) of the AI Act. Removing a safeguard needs a proper decision, not a quick change.

### Question escalation-3
You suspect you put customer data into an unapproved AI tool. Why should you report it today rather than next week?

- [ ] There is no rush; data breaches have no deadlines.
- [x] Personal data breaches must, where feasible, be notified to the authority within 72 hours of the organisation becoming aware, unless unlikely to result in a risk.
- [ ] Reports are only accepted on Mondays.

Explanation: Article 33 GDPR. Early reports give your organisation time to assess the breach and meet the deadline.

### Question escalation-4
Which first step is wrong when something goes wrong with an AI tool?

- [ ] Stop using the output for that task.
- [x] Forward the problematic output to the whole team so everyone knows.
- [ ] Note what tool you used, when, and what went in and out.

Explanation: Do not spread personal data or harmful content further than needed. Report it to the right person instead.

### Question escalation-5
You are not sure whether something you saw is a problem. What should you do?

- [x] Report it. A concern that turns out to be nothing costs little.
- [ ] Wait until you are certain.
- [ ] Ask the AI tool whether it is a problem.

Explanation: Early reports help. The people you report to can decide whether action is needed.

### Question escalation-6
Roles: builders

Your organisation provides a high-risk AI system. What should be ready before it goes live?

- [ ] A marketing launch plan only.
- [x] A process to handle and report serious incidents as required by Article 73 of the AI Act.
- [ ] Nothing; incident processes are set up after the first incident.

Explanation: Providers of high-risk AI systems must report serious incidents to the market surveillance authorities (Article 73). The process must exist before an incident happens.

### Question escalation-7
Roles: managers

A team member reports a concern about an AI tool that turns out to be a false alarm. How should you respond?

- [x] Thank them and keep encouraging reports.
- [ ] Warn them not to waste time.
- [ ] Record it as a performance issue.

Explanation: People stop reporting if reports have negative consequences. A culture of early reporting catches real problems.
